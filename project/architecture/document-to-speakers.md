# From Open to audible speech

This walkthrough follows the integrated reader at `fd1df4c` and identifies what changed in the November 20 pre-alfa build at `30ad838` (draft #169). These are development builds. The running app's outputs depend on the document, its reusable coverage, the selected engine and the installed resources. [Pre-alfa evidence](https://github.com/kellyjanderson/readaloud/issues/158#issuecomment-7050755297)

| Step | What the reader actually does | What can change the result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Open | Flutter accepts a path, bytes or pasted text. The preparation supervisor resolves a source identity and checks for reusable processing. Adapters traverse PDF pages, EPUB spine entries, DOCX document XML or text/HTML blocks. Some container work precedes the first range. | Source format, normalization, range size, reading position, checkpoint identity and available coverage affect the time until readable text. |
| 2. Prepare or restore | Source adapters map display spans to normalized speech and publish ranges. A JSON book (v1–v4) restores prepared data. A portable v5 ZIP has an index, prepared ranges and WAV scenes, and can serve verified audio without synthesis. Saved cast choices and source positions survive compatible reopen. | Source revisions, exact anchors, saved intents and rendition dependencies decide which ranges or scenes can be reused. The UI can request a different position or cancel. |
| 3. Analyze and cast | Dart derives provisional punctuation breaks, capitalization/exclamation emphasis candidates, say-as candidates and dialogue. Optional ModernBookNLP runs a separate Python/Torch worker. Tokens, syntax, quote spans and coreference supply linguistic evidence. The chapter branch saves chapter analysis, uses source-quote corroboration and speaker context, and projects chapters into Fish blocks. | Analysis coverage and provenance, quote boundaries, explicit speaker overrides and voice assignment matter. An entity mentioned in narration does not itself need a speaking voice. The fresh-source chapter wait is a measured startup bottleneck. |
| 4. Plan speech | Dart projects provisionally or specifically attributed spans into ordered render requests. It translates supported pronunciations, resolves cast bindings and groups compatible turns under the model's context and resource limits. The chapter branch uses linguistic scene/context evidence within prepared ranges. | Chunk or scene selection changes model context and phrasing. Voice changes, limits, explicit delivery directions, pronunciation support and whether an engine can handle multiple speakers constrain each request. |
| 5. Resolve or render audio | A compatible cached asset bypasses generation. On a miss, the Dart owner admits bounded work. Fish Drama 3 encodes reference audio/transcript pairs into speaker slots and generates PCM through a resident Python MLX driver on Apple Silicon. The native MLX tensors do the numerical work. Preset and reference engines have different inputs. The **deprecated Kokoro** route drives native ONNX from Dart with phonemes, a voice vector and numeric speed. | Selection, exact model/precision, references, speaker order, token/text payload, translated cues and effective rate affect rendering or its cache identity. Cached audio has its own lifetime beyond document preparation. |
| 6. Assemble and schedule | The boundary worker measures head/tail silence on WAV chunks. On resume the leading quiet cap is 120 ms. Join budgets are 60/120/240/420/650 ms for none/weak/sentence/paragraph/section. Correction trims the incoming quiet head. Internally timed pauses and the previous tail can remain. Fish can also produce ordered PCM packets with render-local frame offsets. | Trim thresholds and requested boundary class affect eligible silence. Queue credit, refill and cancellation determine whether playback can continue. A budget here does not insert that duration of silence into a sentence. |
| 7. Reach speakers or export | The reader hands admitted media to the native player through its Flutter audio adapter. The device output route and clock determine what is emitted. Seeking or replacing a session revokes old playback. Export writes ordered audio separately and can persist portable scenes. | Transport position and output device, OS volume/mute, interruptions, underruns and sample-rate conversion affect playback. Replayable content on disk, enqueued PCM, a software capture and human listening are distinct measurements. |

Source owners include the [intake supervisor](../../lib/src/services/document_preparation_supervisor.dart), [base inference](../../lib/src/services/base_speech_annotation_inference_service.dart), [chapter implementation at the pre-alfa commit](https://github.com/kellyjanderson/readaloud/blob/30ad8385cdb01e67fda894a78df5b8309e5df298/lib/src/controllers/reader_chapter_analysis.dart), [chunk planner](../../lib/src/services/chunk_planner_service.dart), [render worker](../../lib/src/services/mlx_speech_worker_processor.dart), [boundary policy](../../lib/src/services/synthesis_boundary_policy.dart) and [playback owner](playback-orchestration-and-synthesis-boundaries.md).

## Which control changes what you hear?

| Intent | Control that reaches the audio in the traced builds | Limitation to account for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | The stored voice-speed preference applies the session rate across the cast. The deprecated Kokoro model consumes the 0.70×–1.40× value during inference. | The MLX request omits rate. A changed cache key or fresh Fish render cannot establish a speed change. Listening time-stretch and baking a rate into export require distinct contracts. |
| Cadence | Punctuation, sentence/turn boundaries, neighboring context in the same render, the voice model and quiet-head trimming all influence rhythmic delivery. | Syntax can suggest a good phrase break. It supplies neither a duration in frames nor a guarantee that independent generations will join naturally. |
| Emphasis, pitch, expression | Authored intent and heuristic candidates survive preparation. Engine adapters translate supported cues. Scoped bf16 Fish whisper/shout scenes have bounded evidence. | The speaker model supplies much of the actual intonation. There is no verified general pitch or per-word stress dial across engines or precisions. |
| Accent and pronunciation | Reference voice/transcript or preset determines much of the acoustic accent. The lexicon and spoken substitutions can change particular words. The deprecated Kokoro adapter also accepts supported phoneme forms. | A locale tag or same-reference cache key cannot establish a consistent heard accent. On the chapter branch, one reported narrator variation remains open despite a consistent reference. |
